# Intent framework (notional)

An invented framework for taking an idea from a sentence to something a team
could commit to. It has two axes: **depth** (how far along the thinking is)
and **breadth** (the areas the thinking must cover). An idea is as deep as
its shallowest required area.

## Depth

### 1. Spark
A sentence someone believes. Enough to decide whether to spend an hour on it.

### 2. Framed
We can say who it is for, what problem it addresses and what would be
different if it worked. Enough to decide whether to spend a week on it.

### 3. Shaped
Boundaries are drawn: what is in, what is deliberately out, what could sink
it. Enough to ask other teams for their view.

### 4. Tested
The riskiest assumptions have met evidence or the people who would know.
Enough to ask for people and time.

### 5. Committable
A team could start on Monday and a sponsor could say what they are buying.

## Breadth

Each area lists what it takes to reach each depth. "Required from" is the
depth at which the area can no longer be empty.

### Customer
Required from: 1
- 1: Someone is named, even loosely.
- 2: A specific group, and the situation they are in when this matters.
- 3: Who is deliberately not served, and why.
- 4: We have heard it from them, or seen it in their behaviour.
- 5: We know how many there are and how to reach them.

### Problem
Required from: 1
- 1: A problem is stated, not only a solution.
- 2: Stated in the customer's words, with what they do about it today.
- 3: Why it has not been solved already.
- 4: Evidence for it and evidence against it, both looked for.
- 5: A size, with its source.

### Outcome
Required from: 2
- 2: What would be different for the customer if this worked.
- 3: One measure that would move, and which way.
- 4: A baseline for that measure, and a level that would count as success.
- 5: When we would look, and what we would do if it did not move.

### Scope
Required from: 3
- 3: What is in, and at least two things that are deliberately out.
- 4: The smallest version that would still produce the outcome.
- 5: What comes after the first version, and what never will.

### Risks
Required from: 3
- 3: The three things most likely to sink it, each with who would know.
- 4: The riskiest one has been put to whoever would know.
- 5: Each has an owner or an accepted cost.

### Dependencies
Required from: 3
- 3: Which other teams or systems it needs, by name.
- 4: Each has been asked, and has answered.
- 5: Each has agreed a date or said no.

### Cost
Required from: 4
- 4: A rough size from the people who would build it.
- 5: People, time and what they would otherwise be doing.

## Rules

- Never raise an area's depth on the strength of confident wording. It moves
  when the thing its line asks for exists, and you can point to it.
- Ask for one area at a time, the shallowest required one first.
- An area can be marked "not needed" with a reason. That is a decision, and
  it is the person's.
