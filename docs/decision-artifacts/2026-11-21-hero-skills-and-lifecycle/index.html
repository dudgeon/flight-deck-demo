Hero skills and the product lifecycle

You asked for research on the product development lifecycle and where AI helps, to refine the hero skills without splitting the work into too many subskills or making it rigid. I read your product agent and harness library on the Mac mini first, then checked the stage list against the public frameworks. Five decisions follow. Each one is also a card in the thread, asked one at a time.

What I read

Your library (project-agent-and-harness, about 1,500 source notes): the skill rigidity research and its design guide, the PM jobs-to-be-done brief and jobs map, the lifecycle map across nine companies (Stripe, Ramp, Shopify, Anthropic, Atlassian, Intercom, Airbnb, Spotify, Block), the shaping and dependency agents overview, the Pathfinder component design, and your two proposals on the intent factory and on shaping in a regulated setting.

Public frameworks: the Double Diamond, dual-track agile, Torres's continuous discovery and opportunity solution tree, Shape Up, Amazon's Working Backwards, and spec-driven development (Spec Kit, Kiro).

Nearly all evidence about PM agents is first-party or anecdote. The strongest independent evidence is about how a skill treats the person's thinking, and it comes from education and decision-support studies, not product work.

Seven findings

  1. The jobs are stable, and the frameworks agree on four kinds of thinking. Understand the problem, shape a bet, commit and build, learn. Nobody doing well runs more than four or five named phases.
  2. Stages overlap and loop. Dual-track runs discovery and delivery at the same time. Your dependency research shows requirements reworded once their delivery path is known. Stage is a lens on the work, not a place it sits.
  3. Deciding, reviewing and reporting happen at every stage. They are rituals done to an artifact, fixed in form and open in content, so they are poor fits as stages.
  4. A skill is a score, not a dial. Real skills set their freedom step by step. Make a step rigid only where a rule must always hold, and leave the rest to the model. Fix the ritual, free the content.
  5. One skill, one stance. Producing an answer and helping the person think need opposite designs. Merge skills that share a stance, not ones that don't.
  6. AI is best at bounded, reviewable work and worst at choosing. Synthesis with quotes checked against source (Torres found about 30% of a model's quotes wrong or missing), status, first drafts plus gap review. People keep the choice; the agent makes checking cheap.
  7. The unsolved seam is research to intent with the evidence still attached. None of the nine companies documents it. If every skill writes evidence, open questions and decisions to the same initiative, the reasons travel with the work.
Decision 1 of 5 · HS-1

What shape should the hero-skill set take?

Today there are seven workflows, one per stage, plus the lifecycle skill you decided on (D-129). Decide and Review are stages even though they apply to anything.

Keep seven, re-tag stagesLeast work and nothing moves for the scenarios, but Decide and Review stay stuck to one stage and the Library still shows eight things.
Five arcs, two moves, one companionRecommendedPRD to dependencies runs Specify (the catalog), Map (the board) and Choose (alternatives with scope and delivery levers, then a phased roadmap), and you can move between them. Engage owners runs each chosen team's onboarding (intake, access, due diligence and gates, terms, provisioning), with the agent's reach set step by step by who answers: self-serve, another agent, or a person. Review and Decide are offered on any artifact. Problem discovery also takes post-launch feedback, so Learn needs no new skill.
Three arcsFewest names, but one Define skill has to interrogate you and also produce a catalog, which the research says each do worse when mixed.
Something elseTell me the shape you have in mind.
Why B: it splits by your goal and by stance, the two lines the evidence supports. It merges only steps that share a stance and a hand-off. Your Pathfinder design keeps a fit record for every option considered and generates the delivery plan from them, so choosing belongs with mapping, and Choose uses Pathfinder's four kinds of alternative. Onboarding with other teams runs for weeks, through several owners and gates, which is a different goal and stance from choosing. Pathfinder puts it downstream "with their own skills", and your dependency research names three of its seven values there (intake, asking, gate evidence). So it gets its own arc rather than being a move.

Revised 22 November after your atlas comments on where requirement mapping happens, choosing among alternatives, and onboarding with other teams.

Decision 2 of 5 · HS-2

How fixed should each skill's steps be?

Each workflow lists its steps today, and they read as a fixed sequence. The research says to fix only what must always hold.

SkillCheckpoints that always hold
Problem discoveryEvery problem cites a source; every quote is checked against it; you choose what is worth solving
Intent shapingThe agent never writes your answer; every gap has an owner or is marked open; you say when the target depth is reached
PRD to dependenciesEvery requirement traces to a line; every dependency has evidence or is listed "could not find"; asks are drafted, a person sends
Delivery statusEvery date change names its cause and source; the update says what it asks of the reader
Review, DecideEvery finding points to a line and says who raised it; you decide; a decision supersedes, never edits, an earlier one
Keep the fixed step listPredictable demos, but every run walks every step even when you bring half the work already done.
Suggested path plus checkpointsRecommendedSteps become a suggested path; three or so checkpoints per skill always hold and the scenario checks test their structural half.
No steps at allMost flexible, but the progress strip has nothing to show and the honesty rules have no fixed place to fire.
Something elseTell me how much should be fixed.
Why: this is how the good skills you collected work (fix the ritual, free the content), and it fits your strictness levels: light touch skips more suggested steps, by the book walks them all, and the checkpoints never move.
Decision 3 of 5 · HS-3

Which stages, and are they gates?

The lifecycle file lists Discover, Shape, Specify, Map, Decide, Deliver. Nothing after delivery, and Decide sits as one stage though it happens at all of them.

Six tags, Decide becomes a moveRecommendedDiscover, Shape, Specify, Map, Deliver, Learn, as loose tags; a skill may serve several (problem discovery serves Discover and Learn).
Keep today's sixNo change, but nothing covers post-launch learning, the job with the best evidence that agents help.
Four broad stagesUnderstand, Shape, Build, Learn: simpler, but Specify and Map, the core of your PRD example, lose their own place on the strip.
Something elseTell me the stage list you want.
Why: dual-track and continuous discovery both run stages at once, so tags fit how work happens. The data model thread already keeps stage as a loose tag, so this list can change later without changing the model.
Decision 4 of 5 · HS-4

Where do evidence, open questions, decisions and readiness live?

Each skill keeps its own dataset today, so a decision made while shaping is invisible to the dependency board. This is the seam none of the nine companies has solved.

On the initiative, sharedRecommendedOne place for the why; any skill, person or agent sees what is open and decided. The data model thread designs the shape.
In each skill's datasetNo change, but the reasons stay behind in the skill that made them.
Only decisions sharedA smaller step: one shared decision log, while evidence and open questions stay per skill.
Something elseTell me where they should live.
Why: these four are the checkable layer the rigidity research says to make deterministic, and they are what your Pathfinder design keys by stable id. Sharing them is what lets a small skill set feel like one piece of work.
