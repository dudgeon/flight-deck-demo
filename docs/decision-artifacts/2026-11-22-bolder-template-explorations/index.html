Bolder template explorations

Geoff's note: the templates are too conservative. Here are six of the hero skills' core widgets and views, each with today's render beside two bolder directions: A, instruments (readouts, strips, tapes and gauges, in the cockpit's own vocabulary) and B, maps (spatial pictures of the work: networks, radar, branches, terrain). They are all live, built on each skill's real scenario data, use only the design tokens, and work in light and dark.

Adventurous inside the language

Stays fixed

  • Only --pa- tokens: colors, fonts, corners
  • Color means something: navy for the one primary action, amber for needs you, red only for failures, indigo for the agent at work, magenta only for what you did
  • Status always shows an icon, a word and a color
  • Sentence case, with no all-caps eyebrows
  • Light and dark, offline, one self-contained file
  • Chat widgets stay 720 px or narrower and fold once answered

Now free to push

  • A metaphor for each view, drawn from the work itself
  • Drawing the data (gauges, maps, branches, plots) instead of listing it
  • Strong scale contrast: one big number, then the detail
  • Motion that explains a change (a strip slides along, a date flips, a flag moves)
  • Direct manipulation: pick, toggle and drag where that is the point

Rules these would bend ! needs a call

  • Mono for readouts and strip call signs, where the rule today is mono only for ids and code
  • A display size above --pa-text-title (a new --pa-text-display token)
  • Hatched fills for unknowns, such as a planned capability's wait
  • Author hues (--pa-author-n) used as a view's main color in the review sketches

1 · Dependency board

requirements-to-dependenciescanvas

One exploration per requirement, with the options for each dependency. Today it's a five-column kanban. Allowances scenario: 20 requirements, 14 dependencies, 23 options.

A · Flight stripsClick a strip to open its dependencies and options. Start a queued one and watch it move.

Why: air traffic control strips are made for scanning many items at once, ordered by what needs attention. Needs you comes first, the call sign is the requirement id, and fit reads like a fuel gauge.

B · Coverage mapHover or tab to a requirement to trace its path. Filter by capability.

Why: it shows at a glance where the platform already covers you (thick, live paths) and where it doesn't (no path, or a dashed path to a planned capability). That's the question the board is really answering.

2 · Intent map

intent-shapingcanvas

How deep the thinking is in each of seven areas, against a target. Today it's a tree-table with five little bars per row. "Release dates" scenario, now at depth 0 and aiming for 3.

State shown:
A · Altimeter tapesClick a tape to see what the next level asks for.

Why: depth is altitude. The idea flies at the height of its lowest required area, so that tape glows amber and its question is the one asked.

B · RadarThe dashed ring is the target. Hover a spoke.

Why: the shape of the thinking is visible at once. A lopsided polygon shows strong customer work with an empty problem statement before you read a word.

3 · Decision card

decision-reviewchat

One decision, its options, the agent's recommendation and why. Today it's a stack of option boxes. Allowances decisions D-1 and D-2.

A · Fork in the roadPick a branch. Your choice turns magenta and folds.

Why: a decision is a fork. The recommended branch is drawn heavier, so the agent's view is visible without taking the choice away from you.

B · Trade-off gridOptions as columns, consequences as meters.

Why: when the options trade one thing for another, as with speed against promise risk, the meters make the trade legible. It's the same pattern as the alternatives worksheet.

4 · Discovery summary

problem-discoverychat

Where the discovery stands: sources read, problems found, and how strong each one is. Today it's count tiles and bars. New business accounts scenario.

A · Heads-up readoutRings show spread and pain out of 5. Ticks are evidence for and against.

Why: one glance says how much has been read and which problems carry weight. Counter-evidence ticks sit right beside the supporting ones, so nothing hides.

B · Problem terrainClick a problem to read its strongest quote.

Why: plotting how many people have a problem against how much it hurts is the choice the person actually makes. The top right is worth solving, and the quote sits one click away.

5 · Delivery plan

delivery-statuscanvas

Milestones against the plan, as of the moment in the scenario when Cards Platform reports that the load test slips nine days. Today it's a tree-table of dates.

A · Departures boardPress "Report the slip" to watch the board change.

Why: everyone can read a departures board, and the flip draws the eye to exactly what changed.

B · Drift timelineTry each way to recover. The launch flag moves.

Why: the critical path is drawn as a line, and a slip pushes everything after it. Trying a recovery option shows its effect before you choose it.

6 · Review findings

document-reviewcanvas

Everyone's findings on the Allowances PRD in one attributed list. Today it's a table with a column for who raised each.

A · Triage consoleAccept or push back. The tally keeps score.

Why: review is triage. Severity lanes put the high findings first, each reviewer keeps their own color, and your calls show in magenta.

B · Document marginFindings pinned where they land in the PRD. Click a pin.

Why: you see where the document is weak, not only a list of what is wrong. Sections 5 and 7 light up at once.

What I'd like to know

Which direction feels right overall: instruments, maps, or a mix chosen per view? And which of the rule bends above are acceptable? Comments on any sketch are welcome. Whatever you pick becomes a design note in product-aviator-design and new view-kit layouts, starting with the ones the hero skills use most.
