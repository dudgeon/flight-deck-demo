Hangar: where the experiences get built

Product Aviator gets two modes. In one you fly: you do product work with the agent and everything is polished. In the other you are in the hangar: you define the people, workflows, views and scenarios that other people will fly. This page is the design, a clickable mockup, and seven choices for you.

The idea in one screen

Flying

The app as it is today. Navy frame, sessions, the Library, the Session panel. Nothing here hints at files or structure. A person meets Mara and Risk Review as colleagues, and a workflow as a way of working.

Hangar

The same app with its panels off. Drafting-paper ground, dashed edges, a band across the top that says where you are and how to get back. Here Mara is a file with a brief, and a workflow is steps, records and views you can inspect and change.

Three ideas carry the design.

1. Everything in the Hangar is already a file. The roster, the workflow folders, the view specs, the screenplays and the starters exist on disk today and already have checks (pa check, pa who --check). The Hangar is a way of seeing and changing those files. It adds no database and no new format.

2. Building is itself a workflow. "New person", "New workflow", "New scenario" and "New skill" each run as a guided session with Aviator: a few steps, a form in the canvas, a check, a trial, a publish. They use the same step card, the same views and the same chat as any other kind of work. The builder never writes a file by hand unless they choose to.

3. Nothing changes for anyone until Publish. All work happens on a draft copy. Publish runs the checks, shows what will change and who is affected, keeps the previous version, and only then swaps the files in. Running sessions keep what they started with, which the app already does.

What prevents breaking changes

ConstraintHow it shows upThe breakage it prevents
Drafts onlyEdit always opens a copy. The live thing is read-only in the Hangar.A half-finished change reaching someone who is flying.
Pick, do not typePeople, views, record types and layouts are chosen from what exists. Ids are generated from names and never typed.A workflow that names a person or view that does not exist.
Fixed shapesA person needs a name, a kind, a way of answering and a "call for" line. A real person is always marked stand-in. A workflow has three to six steps and uses the standard layouts.Things the app cannot draw, and agents passed off as real people.
Used-by guardRenaming, removing or reshaping something lists everything that depends on it. The change is blocked until each is updated or the builder picks a replacement.Removing Risk Review while four workflows call it. Renaming a button a workflow's instructions rely on.
Built-ins are lockedThe kit and the reference workflows show a lock and "Duplicate to change".Losing the known-good examples everyone copies from.
Publish gatePublish is disabled while a check fails. A scenario must also play through start to finish on its own.A demo that stops halfway in front of an audience.
Always a way backEvery publish keeps the previous version. Restore is one click.A bad change with no undo.

How it gets built, pragmatically

PieceReusesNew
Hangar frameThe app shell, sidebar and design tokens.A /hangar route, a body class that swaps the frame, a second nav list, the band. One extra token family for the Hangar color.
Shelves and inspect pagesThe Library endpoint pattern. Participant marks. The HTML viewer for rendered views.A read-only endpoint that lists roster, workflows, views, scenarios, skills, starters and how they refer to each other.
Guided buildsSessions, the step card, view-kit forms, the participants and scenario-builder skills.Four builder skills, each a workflow package of its own. A "Hangar" project so builder sessions stay out of the flying sidebar.
Drafts, publish, historyFiles on disk.pa draft, pa publish, pa history: copy to a drafts folder, check, snapshot, copy into place, restore.
Cross-checkspa check, pa who --check.pa uses <thing>: what depends on this, used by the used-by guard and the inspect pages.

Two things I decided without asking, because they are cheap to change: versions are dated snapshot folders (they work from a ZIP download, where git may not exist), and a person's file gains an optional skills line so the Hangar can show and limit which skills a person's session gets. Sharing a workflow or scenario with another PM as a single file is left for later.
