Conversation Participants
Product Aviator · interaction primitive

Other agents and other people, in the same conversation

Today the chat has two voices: you and your Aviator. This page works through how a risk agent, a platform agent or a designer joins that conversation as a participant in their own right, and how the screen says who said and did what.

Try it

One conversation, three ways to show it

Your example, played out: you ask for a PRD review, Aviator calls a Risk Review agent, then asks your designer one question. Switch the treatment to compare. Data and names are invented.

The primitive

A participant is a small file

Everything on this page hangs off one new thing: a participant. It has a name, a kind, a role, a mark, and a brief that says how it behaves. Agents, platforms and people are all described the same way, so the screen can treat them the same way.

What the file buys

Kind decides the shape of the mark: square for agents, round for people.

stand_in marks a person who is being played by an agent. The screen always shows it, so a demo never passes off a simulated colleague as a real one.

source says where the turns come from. Changing it does not change the screen.

Mechanism

Four places a participant's turns can come from

The screen needs only one thing from any of them: each turn says which participant it belongs to. That lets a demo start scripted and move to real agents without redesign.

Neither merges a second agent's replies into your timeline, which is the part that would be new.

Beyond the chat

Attribution has to follow the work

Comments in a documentEach comment carries its author's mark. Omnigent's comments already have authors, so this is a matter of letting an agent participant be one.
Risk Review · "No stated limit on how often the amount can change."
Views in the canvasA view shows who opened it, and the tab carries their mark. A participant's widget in chat sits inside their turn.RRRisk register
Records in the datasetEach record keeps who created it and who last changed it. Views can then filter to "what Risk Review added" and show the mark beside a finding.
What only you can doGuests propose. Approving, locking and sending stay yours, exactly as now. A guest's change to your records arrives as a suggestion in their color.

Recommendation

Named turns always, wrapped in a block when someone is called in

Use B as the default, built on A. Every turn that is not yours or Aviator's carries a name and mark. When Aviator calls someone in, their visit is one block with a start, an end and a one-line result, so a twenty-turn risk review folds down and the main thread stays yours.

Keep C as the overflow: any block can be popped out into the side conversation Omnigent already has, for long back-and-forth.

For the mechanism, start with scripted turns in the previews and sub-agents with a persona in the live app. Both need the same small change: the app learns to draw a marked turn as a participant. Separate sessions come when the work team builds the real thing.

Still open

Questions this raises

Who may call whom? In the example Aviator proposes the call and makes it. Should it ask first every time, or only for people?

Can a guest see everything? A sub-agent gets what Aviator passes it. A real separate agent would need rules for what context it receives.

Can you call a participant yourself? Typing @Risk Review in the message box is the obvious way, and it would make the roster a place to browse who is available.

How honest must a stand-in be? I have assumed a simulated person is always labelled as one, in every treatment. If a demo needs the illusion, that should be a deliberate switch.
