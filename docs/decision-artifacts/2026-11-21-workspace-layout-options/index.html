Where do outputs and context live when the right side is also the canvas?

Five ways to place a session's outputs and its context, drawn at wireframe level. Each is shown twice: while you are only chatting, and with the canvas open.

navigationchatcanvasoutputs and context
The tension

Two things want the same space

A list of what this session has made and what it may read is small, always relevant and wants to be glanceable. The canvas is large, wants every pixel, and is where structured work happens. On a 13-inch laptop there is room for navigation, chat and one more column. So the real question is which of the two gets that column, and where the other one goes.

Split by direction

Recommended

Where other participants fit

Other agents and people will join the conversation as participants (see Conversation Participants). That adds a third small list beside outputs and context: who is in the room. It does not change the ranking, but it sharpens it.

Where the roster goesEffect
A Panel gives wayA "People and agents" section in the Session panelHidden whenever the canvas is open, which is when guests are most active
B Home tabOn the home tabSame problem: not visible beside the work
C Left sidebarA third group under context and outputsAlways visible, at the cost of width
D Thin stripA fourth icon, or the marks themselves stacked on the stripMarks on the strip work well as presence
E Split by directionMarks in the chat header, and the address chip in the message boxFits the rule: who you talk to sits where you talk

In every option the chat header should show the marks of who is present, because attribution in the timeline only reads well if the reader has already met the cast.

Recommendation

Ship A now, build toward E

A is in the branch tonight because it reuses a panel Omnigent already has. It answers "can I see outputs and choose context at all" with almost no risk.

E is where I would take it. Context is an input, so it belongs beside the message box, where you can see what the agent will read before you press Send. Outputs are what the canvas shows, so the canvas should list them itself. That removes the tension instead of managing it: nothing competes with the canvas for the right side.

C is the one to revisit when context becomes project-wide and shared across sessions, because a tree in the sidebar is the natural shape for that.
